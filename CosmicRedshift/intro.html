Cosmic Redshift

How Expanding Space Turns Blue Light Invisible

Welcome to an interactive exploration of cosmic redshift — the reason the earliest galaxies in the Universe can only be photographed by infrared telescopes, and never by optical ones. Over the next three pages you will follow a single pulse of blue light from a star that formed 450 million years after the Big Bang, all the way to a detector on Earth 13.3 billion years later.

What You’ll Discover:
  • Why expanding space stretches a light wave — and why this is not the Doppler effect
  • How to read the redshift number z, and the one formula that runs the whole module
  • Where along its 13.3 billion light-year journey the pulse stops being visible
  • Why the star that emitted this light died long before the light arrived — and what that says about Olbers’ Paradox
