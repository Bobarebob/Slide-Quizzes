Connection to Olbers’ Paradox
If the Universe were infinite, eternal, and static, every line of sight would eventually land on a star and the night sky would blaze. It does not. Why is the night sky dark?

This animation shows you two of the reasons at once.

The Universe has a finite age. Light from beyond about 13.8 billion light-years has not had time to arrive at all. There is a horizon.

Expansion redshifts what does arrive. A vast amount of starlight reaches Earth every second having been stretched clean out of the visible band. It is here. Your eyes simply have no receptor for it.

🤔 The unsettling part: some of the light bathing the night sky right now was emitted by objects that ceased to exist billions of years ago. The photon outlives its source. You are looking at a sky partly furnished by ghosts — and mostly in colors you cannot see.
