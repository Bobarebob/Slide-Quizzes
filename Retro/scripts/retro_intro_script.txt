Planetary Retrograde Motion (QUIZ_ID: RETRO01)
Target length: about 1 minute 45 seconds.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

Welcome to Planetary Retrograde Motion.

Every so often, a planet appears to stop its normal drift against the
stars, back up for a few weeks, then move forward again. For centuries
that backward loop was the hardest problem in astronomy. By the end of
this exercise, you will be able to explain it in one sentence.

You have three objectives. 

First, you’ll learn why the Earth-centered model needed circles within 
circles, called epicycles and deferents, and why Copernicus’ Sun-centered 
model did not. 

Second, you’ll be able to explain the retrograde motion of Mars, an outer 
planet that Earth overtakes on the inside track. 

Third, you’ll be able to explain the retrograde motion of Venus, a planet that laps Earth from the inside. The geometry differs in the two cases, and that difference matters.

Now, the controls.

Every page has a voiceover panel with Start, Stop and Resume. Use it.

The Mars and Venus pages carry the retrograde animation, with four
buttons beside it. Pause and Resume freezes the motion so you can study
a single moment. Slow Down reduces the orbital speeds, and it is probably 
the most useful button on the two page. Reset returns both planets to their starting positions. Fullscreen enlarges the animation; Escape brings you back.

While the animation runs, watch three things. A yellow sight line extends from
Earth, through the planet, out to the star field. A colored dot on the outer 
star ring marks where that planet appears in your night sky. And
that dot changes color: green, when the planet appears to move forward, or
prograde, and red, when the planet appears to move backward, or retrograde.

Six questions, two per content page. Each answer locks the moment you
click it and tells you immediately whether you were right or wrong, so read every
option before choosing.

Move between pages with the Next and Previous buttons, or jump between pages using the dots at the bottom.
