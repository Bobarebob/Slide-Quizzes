Twilight (Definition)
Twilight is the interval between sunset and the moment the Sun reaches 18° below the horizon. That vertical drop is the same everywhere on Earth. What changes from place to place is how long the Sun takes to cover it.

At the equator on an equinox, the Sun goes down like a stone — straight down, leaning 0° from the vertical. Total twilight is 72 minutes. Move north and the path tilts: the Sun slides down and to the right as it sets, so it covers the same 18° of vertical drop more slowly. At London the same descent takes 119 minutes.

💡 One more thing, and it matters for page 3: on an equinox the Sun sets due west no matter where you stand. Only the angle of the descent changes, never the direction. Check it on the azimuth diagram — 270.0°, 0° off due west, at every latitude you try.
